India's grid is cleanest at noon.

Grid-India publishes all-India generation by fuel every 15 minutes. CleanHour turns that into carbon intensity per hour, and schedules heavy AI jobs into the cleanest (and, on the power exchange, cheapest) window.

The scheduler

Give it a job length and a deadline. It forecasts the next 24 hours from the same hour over the past week, uses real IEX prices where the day-ahead market has already cleared, and starts the job in the best window.

Forecast gCO₂/kWh, next 24hChosen 2-hour window
$ python -m cleanhour plan --hours 2 --kwh 50
Best window : Fri 30 Oct 11:00 -> 13:00  ~511 gCO2/kWh, Rs 0.88/kWh

$ python -m cleanhour run --hours 2 --within 24 -- python train.py
[cleanhour] cleanest 2h window starts Sat 11:00 (~506 gCO2/kWh); waiting 0.8h
[cleanhour] starting: python train.py

Method & caveats

  • Data. Grid-India (NLDC) daily Power Supply Position reports, "TimeSeries" sheet: all-India MW by fuel every 15 minutes. Prices: IEX Day-Ahead Market hourly clearing price (MCP).
  • Intensity. Direct combustion only. Coal and lignite at 0.96 tCO₂/MWh (CEA CO₂ Baseline Database, operating margin), gas at 0.45. Solar, wind, hydro and nuclear count as zero. Storage discharge counts as zero to avoid double counting. "Others" also counts as zero. Lifecycle factors would raise every hour slightly but barely change the shape.
  • National average. This is the all-India mix. A specific data centre's state grid and power contracts will differ.
  • Laptop energy. GPU power is measured through NVML. CPU and RAM are CodeCarbon estimates, because Windows exposes no RAPL counters. The job uses the same kWh at any hour; only the CO₂ and price per kWh change.
  • Price. The DAM price is what a large consumer buying on the exchange pays. A laptop on a flat home tariff pays the same at every hour.
